# Stock vs perf arkworks vs current main, x86 CPU and CUDA: A/B/C, 2026-10-12

The x86 and CUDA runs that `arkff-perf-ab-2026-10-11.md` could not do, with a third build added.
Three snarkrs builds, one toolchain, one set of flags (`bench/scripts/arkab-build.sh`):

- **stock**: 4d90f7b (the prover source of 96340d7) on arkworks tag `v0.5.0` (7ad88c4), whose
  `src` is identical to the crates.io 0.5.0 packages. The binaries of the 2026-10-11 report,
  reused unchanged.
- **perf**: the same source on `perf/bn254-field-0.5` (651414b): row-shaped mont mul/square,
  branch-free add/sub/double/neg, `sum_of_products`. Also reused unchanged.
- **main**: 1f85bd4, on the same `v0.5.0` checkout as stock. It adds GLV scalar multiplication
  on raw limbs (verify's public-input sum, ceremony point multiplications), batch-affine GLV
  `mul_batch` (key scaling, prepare's twiddles), NTT butterflies on `RawFr`, and parallel
  `ptau prepare` passes. Its aarch64 assembly does not build on x86.

x86 builds are `cargo zigbuild --release --target x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu`, `-C
target-feature=+adx,+bmi2`, `--no-default-features --features cpu` (x86) or `cpu,cuda` (CUDA).
`cargo tree -d` shows no duplicate ark crate in any build. main's `field_ops` binary is
byte-identical to stock's (same arkworks, and the example does not use `crates/field`).

Runner `bench/scripts/arkab.py`, three variants, the order reversing each round (stock, perf,
main, then main, perf, stock), 3 rounds. Same steps and statistics as the Mac report: prove is
`snarkrs bench --mode warm`, 10 reps per round, every rep self-verified, cell = median of all 30;
verify is `verify_time`, 500 in-process calls per round after a tampered-signal rejection; each
ceremony step is one CLI process's wall clock, counted only on exit 0 and, for verifiers, their
OK line; cells are the median of the 3 round medians. Changes are against stock. Each run was one
disposable on-demand instance (`bench/aws/arkab-ec2.py`), nothing else on it.

## Findings

- **perf (patched arkworks) behaves on x86 as on the Mac, a bit stronger for prove and verify,
  weaker for the ceremony.**
  - Prove -0.4% to -11%: the NTT is 20 to 25% faster (anon-aadhaar 487 to 390 ms, the 2^18
    circuits about 51 to 39 ms), pointwise 14 to 21%. The MSM does not move. The gain again
    scales with the NTT's share: anon-aadhaar -11%, keccak256 -9%, the MSM-bound 2^15 circuits
    0 to -3%.
  - Verify -25 to -36%, the largest of any backend (Fq2 mul 75.9 to 53.1 ns in `field_ops`).
  - Ceremony gains are smaller than on the Mac: contribute -14% (Mac -22%), setup -5% (Mac
    -18%), zkey contribute -6% (Mac -20%).
  - `field_ops` on the c7i, single threaded: Fr mul 19.2 to 17.8 ns, G1 mixed add 279 to 244
    ns, G2 mixed add 966 to 719 ns. **Fr add throughput regresses here too, 1.42 to 2.82 ns**
    (latency improves, 5.59 to 4.11). Same signature as the Mac regression.
- **main is a ceremony change. On x86 it is 2 to 3 times faster than stock on every
  ceremony step that does point multiplication, and much faster than perf.**
  - `ptau prepare` -43% at 2^13 and -65% at 2^18 (parallel passes and `mul_batch` twiddles).
    perf does not touch prepare at all.
  - `ptau contribute` -33% (2^13) and -64% (2^19); `zkey contribute` -50 to -55% on every
    circuit; `groth16 setup` -30 to -82% (largest on keccak256, railgun, tornado, where the
    point multiplication share is largest; js_16x16_d32 and js_2x2_d32 -30%, their time is
    elsewhere).
  - The verifiers (`ptau verify`, `zkey verify init`) do not move: they are MSM and I/O.
- **main's prove on x86 is slower than stock on the 2^18+ circuits, +1.3 to +4.2%, and the
  cause is the `RawFr` NTT.** The NTT stage is 51.5 to 55.2 ms (js_16x16_d32), 50.0 to 56.5
  (rsa2048), 487 to 528 ms (anon-aadhaar), so +7 to +13%, while MSM, gather and pointwise match
  stock. The raw-limb CIOS and select-based add/sub that win on aarch64 lose to ark-ff's x86
  path here. Stock arkworks with perf's patch has the fastest x86 NTT of the three (38 to 39 ms
  at 2^18), so on x86 main gives up both perf's gain and some of stock. The 2^15 circuits are
  0.2 to 4.4% faster on main (MSM-bound; tornado's MSM is 5% faster). Worth a look before relying on `RawFr` for x86: an x86 mul
  (mulx/adx) for raw.rs, or keeping x86 on the ark-ff butterflies.
- **main's verify gain depends on the public-input count**: -31% on js_16x16_d32 and -23% on
  railgun-13x01 (many public inputs, GLV public-input sum), -3% to -10% elsewhere. perf beats it
  on 8 of 9 circuits because its gain is in the pairing, which main does not change. The two are
  independent, so main on perf arkworks should get both (not measured).
- **CUDA prove does not move for either build (within ±0.8%).** Field math is in the kernels,
  as on Metal. The CPU parts of the CUDA host show the same pattern as x86 on a slower CPU:
  verify -27% (perf) and -2 to -31% (main), setup -5% (perf) and -38 to -88% (main), zkey
  contribute -4% (perf) and -53 to -59% (main), ptau contribute 2^13 -8% (perf) and -56% (main).
- **CUDA `ptau prepare`**: -36% at 2^13 on main, no change at 2^18 (28.5 s on all three). The
  group FFT runs on the GPU; at 2^18 it dominates, at 2^13 the CPU passes main parallelised are a
  larger share. CPU main on x86 takes 113 s at 2^18, so the GPU path still wins by 4x.

## Correctness

- Every timed proof self-verified: 810 warm proofs per backend (9 circuits x 10 reps x 3 rounds
  x 3 builds). The runner recorded 0 failures in the final results of either run (see Caveats
  for the x86 round-1 phase-2 restart).
- `verify_time` rejected a tampered public signal for every circuit and build before timing.
- Our verifier accepted every ceremony output: `ptau verify` on every contribute output,
  `zkey verify init` on every contributed key.
- snarkjs 0.7.2 (the CLI on PATH; the Mac report's "0.7.6" label was hardcoded in
  `arkab_report.py`, which now prints the version it ran): **x86 39 of 39 accepted, CUDA 39 of
  39**: 27 proofs, each build's contributed and prepared 2^13 ptau, each build's contributed
  js_2x2_d32 and js_16x16_d32 zkeys (`zkey verify` against r1cs and ptau).
- **Byte-identical deterministic outputs across all three builds and every round: x86 21 of 21
  (123 files hashed), CUDA 20 of 20 (120 files).** These are `groth16 setup` for all 8 circuits,
  `ptau prepare` at 2^13 and 2^18, `ptau beacon` (2^13 and, on x86, 2^19), `zkey beacon` for all
  8 circuits, and the prove trace. So main's new prepare, setup and beacon paths, and the CUDA
  prepare, write exactly the stock bytes.

## Caveats

- **Phase 2 covers 8 circuits, not 9.** anon-aadhaar needs `ppot_0080_21.ptau` (2.4 GB) and a
  238 MB r1cs; the bundles were sized to leave it out, as the 2026-10-11 x86 bundle did. It is
  in prove and verify.
- **x86 round-1 phase 2 was redone.** The bundle was tarred on macOS without
  `COPYFILE_DISABLE`, so AppleDouble `._*.r1cs` files reached the guest and `groth16 setup`
  picked `._circuit.r1cs` first: all 24 round-1 setup calls failed, before any timing was
  recorded for them. The runner was stopped during round-2 prove, the `._*` files deleted, the
  24 failures moved to `failures-appledouble-r1.json`, and `arkab.py --resume` continued:
  round-1 phase 2 ran then, after round-2 prove, then rounds 2 and 3 in order. The CUDA bundle
  went up with `COPYFILE_DISABLE=1` and had no such files.
- `arkab-ec2.py`'s launch command put `&` on the whole `&&` list, so the launching ssh held
  until the guest finished and progress was not streamed. Harmless, but one dropped connection
  would have ended the run. Fixed to `;` before the CUDA run.
- CUDA `ptau prepare` round 1 stock at 2^13 took 672 s: the cold NVRTC and driver JIT compile
  of the group FFT unit (cached on disk afterwards). The median ignores it; rounds 2 and 3 are
  3.97 s.
- CUDA phase 1 has 2^13 contribute only (2^19 contribute is a CPU step, covered on x86).
- One instance per backend, one run each. Instances share no work, so there is no `--max-load`
  gate and no `*` marks.
