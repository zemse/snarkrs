# CPU or CUDA per circuit on a T4 host, 2026-11-11

Lane S of CUDA lane round 4: where the CPU stops beating CUDA on a T4 host, warm and one
proof per process, variable and constant work, and the `--backend auto` policy that follows.
Main `5c86eb2` (lane K's constant-work MSM) plus `--backend auto`.

Two boxes, binary zigbuilt with `+adx,+bmi2`, driver 595: a g4dn.xlarge (4 vCPU = 2 cores x
2 threads, Xeon 8259CL) and a g4dn.2xlarge (8 vCPU = 4 x 2, same CPU), same T4. Every cell
is `snarkrs bench --backend B --variant ...` on all logical cores, every proof verified:
`--mode warm --reps 10` and `--mode cold-process --reps 5` (one fresh `groth16 prove`
process per proof, what the CLI costs), median ms. cpu and cuda come from one session per
box and work mode, auto from a later one; the near-crossover cells were re-run beside auto
and agree within 2% (below).

The ladder is `bench/artifacts` plus three circuits made for the gap between tiny_mul and
js_1x1_d8, where the warm crossover is: `chain_N` is N constraints
`s[i + 1] <== s[i] * s[i] + i + 3`, domain 128, 512 and 2048, every wire live.

## Summary

- **Warm, the CPU wins only below domain 2^9 to 2^11.** CUDA proves anything past tiny_mul
  in 12 to 15 ms (variable) and 10 to 19 ms (constant work) at the small end; the 8-thread
  CPU catches that at about 1,500 points (chain_500 is 10.1 ms CPU against 13.2 CUDA,
  chain_2000 29.8 against 14.8), the 4-thread one at about 750.
- **One proof per process, the crossover is 200x higher.** CUDA's process pays its context,
  kernel load (warm caches) and key upload: 0.46 s for tiny_mul, 0.55 s under constant work.
  On the 2xlarge the CPU wins every circuit up to rsa2048 and keccak256 (variable) and up to
  js_8x8_d32 (constant work); on the xlarge up to sha256 in both.
- **What predicts it is the CPU's work, not the constraint count.** The CPU MSMs skip witness
  entries that are 0 or 1, and keccak256 and sha256 have no others. Counting the domain size
  plus the live wires (variable) or every wire (constant work), over the rayon threads,
  separates every measured cell with one threshold per mode on both boxes.
- **The policy**: CUDA above 192 points a thread warm (either work mode), 40,000 one-shot
  variable, 32,000 one-shot constant work. `auto` picks the faster backend (or one within
  5% of it) on every measured circuit, box and mode. Same-session, auto costs at most +1.1%
  over the backend it picked on every cell of 30 ms or more (anon-aadhaar one-shot, the
  backend is built after the key load instead of before); one-shot cells under 30 ms are
  process start and swing -3.5% to +6% by round with no consistent sign. Every CLI proof
  under `--backend auto` verified.
- **The default stays `cpu`.** `auto` is opt-in: making it the default would put a CUDA
  context in every proof of a CUDA build without the user naming it, and change which
  device a script's existing command line uses.

## Why the CLI policy is the one-shot one

`groth16 prove` and `fullprove` are one proof per process, so the CLI decides with the
one-shot thresholds, and `bench --mode cold-process` (which runs that CLI) and
`cold-inprocess` (backend and prepare per proof) do too. `bench --mode warm` decides with the
warm thresholds, which is what a resident service built on the library would use. Using the
warm thresholds in the CLI would send sha256 to a 0.5 s CUDA proof when the CPU does it in
0.13 s; using the one-shot ones warm would keep sha256 on a 98 ms CPU proof when CUDA does
it in 15 ms.

## Same-session check of the one-shot overhead

Interleaved on the xlarge, 3 rounds, each round every backend once (10 reps tiny_mul and
sha256, 3 reps js_16x16_d32 and anon-aadhaar), median of round medians, ms:

| circuit | picked | picked backend | auto | auto / picked |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `tiny_mul` | cpu | 9.5 | 10.3 | +8.4% |
| `sha256` | cpu | 227.8 | 228.3 | +0.2% |
| `js_16x16_d32` | cuda | 719.1 | 722.2 | +0.4% |
| `anon-aadhaar` | cuda | 1,606.3 | 1,624.3 | +1.1% |

tiny_mul's 0.8 ms was `auto` reading `rayon::current_num_threads()`, which builds the global
pool, a cost a tiny CPU proof never pays otherwise. `auto` now reads the pool size without
building it (`--threads`, else `RAYON_NUM_THREADS`, else the logical cores). The CUDA
cells pay for building the backend after the key load rather than before it, which `auto`
cannot avoid: it needs the key and the witness to choose.

After the fix, same box, 10 reps a round, median of round medians, ms. The first is 5
rounds of cpu, auto; the second 10 rounds of cpu, auto, cpu again (an A/A arm for the
noise):

| circuit | work | cpu | auto | auto / cpu | cpu again / cpu |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `tiny_mul` | var | 9.14 | 9.16 | +0.1% | |
| `tiny_mul` | cw | 9.23 | 9.23 | 0.0% | |
| `chain_120` | var | 14.93 | 15.72 | +5.3% | |
| `tiny_mul` | var | 9.34 | 9.90 | +6.0% | +2.0% |
| `chain_120` | var | 15.94 | 15.38 | -3.5% | +0.7% |
| `chain_500` | var | 29.13 | 28.77 | -1.2% | -0.6% |

A one-shot proof under 30 ms is mostly process start, and its round medians move by a ms
between rounds whichever backend flag ran: auto against cpu goes from -3.5% to +6.0% with no
consistent sign, and pooled over all 100 reps of the second run tiny_mul is 9.66 auto
against 9.37 and 9.47 for the two cpu arms (+3.1%, +2.0%). No cell of 30 ms or more is
outside 2% in a same-session pair.

## CLI proofs

`snarkrs g16p ... --backend auto --stage-timings -v`, then `snarkrs g16v`, on both boxes,
variable and `--constant-work`, for tiny_mul, chain_500, sha256, js_8x8_d32, keccak256 and
anon-aadhaar: 24 proofs, all `OK!`. The xlarge proved js_8x8_d32 and keccak256 on CUDA in
both modes, the 2xlarge on the CPU except keccak256 under constant work; tiny_mul, chain_500
and sha256 went to the CPU and anon-aadhaar to CUDA on both, as the tables say they should.

## The policy

`bin/snarkrs/src/auto.rs`. Points = `domain_size` plus the witness entries other than 0 and
1 (variable work) or `n_vars` (constant work); per thread = points / rayon pool size. CUDA
when the build has it and per thread is over:

| | variable | constant work |
|---|---:|---:|
| warm (`bench --mode warm`) | 192 | 192 |
| one-shot (CLI, `cold-process`, `cold-inprocess`) | 40,000 | 32,000 |

- Each threshold sits inside the gap between the measured CPU-faster and CUDA-faster
  circuits on both boxes: warm between chain_500 and chain_2000 at 8 threads (126 and 506
  a thread) and chain_120 and chain_500 at 4 (62 and 253); one-shot variable between
  rsa2048 (33,424) and js_16x16_d32 (50,054) at 8 threads and sha256 (16,384) and
  js_8x8_d32 (50,111) at 4; one-shot constant work between js_8x8_d32 (25,214) and
  js_16x16_d32 (50,371) at 8 threads and tornado (15,267) and js_8x8_d32 (50,428) at 4.
- Per thread is what carries one threshold across the two boxes: the CPU proof is 1.8 to
  2.0x faster on the 2xlarge and the CUDA proof the same on both. A host with many more cores
  scales worse than linearly, so `auto` leans to the CPU there; the thresholds are a T4
  host's, and a faster GPU moves them down.
- `auto` never picks Metal or wgpu, is the CPU in a build without `cuda`, and goes to the
  CPU with a warning when the CUDA device does not open. A named `--backend` always runs.
  `-v` prints the choice, and `--stage-timings` prints `backend cpu (auto)`.
- `auto_picks_the_faster_backend_on_every_measured_circuit` in `auto.rs` holds every cell of
  the four tables above against the policy.
